# Developing PumaBass

How PumaBass is put together, how it's tested, and the greykit.games rules it follows. For playing it, see
[README.md](README.md). [`PRODUCT.md`](PRODUCT.md) and [`DESIGN.md`](DESIGN.md) describe the product and the
look, and [`DETECTION.md`](DETECTION.md) what Rocksmith 2014's own files say about its timing and detection.

## Layout

- `index.html`: the page (head tags generated by greykit-meta's `head-tags.sh`)
- `web/js/app.js`: starts the page and routes the keys; the page's parts are its own modules:
  - `core.js`: what every part shares (elements, the song's audio and mixer, the highway, the
    instrument input, which screen is up, the song library);
  - `store.js`: what is kept in the browser; `api.js`: the server;
  - `menu.js`: the title, Settings and Help; `library-screen.js`: the song list and songs folder;
  - `player.js`: the player and its frame loop, with `repeater.js` (the riff repeater),
    `end-screen.js`, `takes.js`, `lyrics.js` (the words at the top, a line at a time) and
    `sync-test.js` (the sync test's flow);
  - `instrument.js`: choosing the input, hearing it, the mix and the tuner
- `web/js/calibrate.js`: the sync test itself
- `web/js/highway.js`: the 3D note highway, and the camera path planned for each song
- `web/js/notation.js`: the notation band and tab view, drawn by [VexFlow](https://www.vexflow.com/) 5.0.0
  (MIT), kept in `web/vendor/vexflow/` so it works without the internet
- `web/js/chart.js`: difficulty levels, tuning names
- `web/js/demos.js`: the demo song, `demo-copybook.js` (*Gods of the Copybook Headings*, Suno AI's
  setting of Kipling's poem), played from its own recording (`web/audio/copybook.m4a`, which goes up
  with the web demo), its bass part and its words' timing transcribed from that recording and written
  out as data. It's on the song list's Demo tab, and the player gets its chart and audio in the browser
  instead of asking the server.
- `web/js/input.js`, `web/js/tuner.js`, `web/js/pitch.js`: the instrument input, the tuner, and its
  pitch detection
- `web/js/scoring.js`, `web/js/capture-worklet.js`: scoring, and the input recorded for it
- `web/js/title.js`: the canon boot and title screen; `painting.js` plays its backdrop (the painting in `web/art/`);
  `scene.js` is the song list's and end screen's stage; `help.js` holds Help and About
- `server/psarc.js`, `server/sng.js`: read Rocksmith `.psarc` archives and `.sng` charts
- `server/library.js`: finds songs in any folder (PC or Mac), song index, chart conversion (with the song's
  lyrics, from the vocals arrangement beside it in the archive), audio lookup and decoding
- `server/server.js`: the local web server, settings, the folder picker, takes. It only hands the browser
  `index.html`, its icons and manifest, and `web/`, and only answers to `localhost` unless `host` says otherwise.
- `tools/build-web.sh`: builds the web demo (below)
- `start.sh`, `start.bat`; `tools/get-vgmstream.sh` (the vgmstream release is pinned there; its Linux
  build is x86-64, so on ARM build vgmstream from source); `tools/pumabass.service`
- `screenshots/`: the README's pictures (the two `.webp`s are animated)

## Checks

CI's validate stage (`.gitlab-ci.yml`) runs the checks that need no songs:

```bash
node tools/check-assets.js
node tools/check-syntax.mjs
node tools/check-pitch.mjs
node tools/check-sync.mjs
node tools/check-plucks.mjs
node tools/check-demo.mjs
```

- `check-pitch.mjs` tests the pitch detector on 2,597 synthesized bass and guitar notes (stiff strings,
  weak fundamentals, noise, fading plucks): every reading the tuner shows is within 2 cents, nearly all
  within 1.
- `check-sync.mjs` tests the sync test (`web/js/calibrate.js`).
- `check-demo.mjs` checks the demo song: its chart is shaped as the player needs (every note in its
  phrase and under the hand's position, an easy level below 50% Notes), its words are in order, and its
  recording is there.
- `check-plucks.mjs` checks that a low note's waveform, which spikes once a period, never counts as a
  pluck, from a 5-string's low B to the 12th fret.

These need songs, so they run locally:

- `node tools/check-scoring.mjs` plays synthesized performances of six songs from your library (human
  timing, stiff strings, weak fundamentals, plus skipped notes, wrong frets, the octave above, and badly
  timed plucks), a timing sweep at full and 70% speed, and rests over mains hum. On 2,079 notes: 98.8% of
  right notes score as hits (hammer-ons and pull-offs 93%, chords 100%); wrong frets get through 0% of the
  time, skipped notes 1%, the octave above 6%, hum under a rest (with a soundcheck) 0%; the timing windows
  are exact. `--sloppy` plays like a learner through a real cable (dragging, notes left ringing, soft
  plucks, an open string ringing) to keep scoring forgiving.
- `node tools/check-camera.mjs` measures the highway's camera path across your library, after any tuning.

## How scoring works

- The input is recorded continuously on the audio thread (`web/js/capture-worklet.js`), stamped with
  the audio clock, so judging doesn't depend on the screen's frame rate.
- A pluck is a jump in the input's high end against the stretch just before it, averaged over more than
  a period of the lowest string.
- Each pluck goes to its nearest note, so a fast run can't lend one note's pluck to another, and a note
  still ringing from before never counts again.
- The soundcheck (in Sync) measures the cable's noise floor as Rocksmith does (its typical level plus
  2 dB); scoring then takes nothing within 10 dB of it for a note, so mains hum can't pass for a low B.
- After a pluck, the pitch is checked with everything from a fourth below the note filtered out: open
  strings left ringing (the low E and A under an F on the D string) blur its period otherwise, and a
  right note reads as a wrong one. In a learner's take, that was 28 of 37 misses.
- The same note again with no click heard (a soft re-pluck of a string still ringing) counts as plucked
  where the note is steady before the finger came down and after, but what follows doesn't carry on what
  came before: out of step or jumped in level, or (an open string re-plucked carries on nearly in step)
  at least twice the energy; never with the level dropping (that's damping). On a learner's takes of
  songs full of repeated notes, that was 53 of 88 and 79 of 151 "already sounding" misses; a string left
  ringing faked it 4 times in 205. That gives its notes a timing.
- Otherwise, a plucked note with no pluck heard gets the benefit of the doubt: it counts if its pitch is
  there at the end of its window (by then whatever was played instead has taken over from what rang
  before). So the same note again counts while it rings, played or not: a fast run of one note (eighths
  on the low E) leaves no room to tell, and what it would take a cleverer detector to call wrong is close
  enough to right. "Already sounding" now only fails a hammer-on or pull-off. check-scoring reports a
  skipped repeat of a note still ringing apart, uncounted. In a fast run the end of the window runs into
  the next note's pluck (soft, unheard, up to 0.1 s early): unless the end clearly says right or wrong,
  the latest look over before then counts instead (Thunderstruck's eighths: 720 to 770 of 968).

**Takes.** With **Record a take** ticked in the Mix panel, **Save take** (there, or `S` on the end screen)
writes what the cable heard, with the song's notes and each verdict, to `takes/` (git ignores it). A song's
worth of input is ~100 MB of memory, so nothing is recorded unless it's ticked. Replay one through scoring
exactly as it ran, with why each miss was a miss, note by note and by string and fret:

```bash
node tools/replay-take.mjs "takes/<name>.json" --notes
```

## How the notation is written

Each note is fitted to the chart's own beats (16ths, or triplets where they fit a beat better), its pitch
is worked out from the string, fret and tuning, and the key signature is the one that leaves the fewest
accidentals. Bass is written an octave above where it sounds, in bass clef; guitar likewise in treble clef.
Rocksmith's timings aren't perfectly on the grid, so now and then a rhythm comes out a little differently
from how you'd write it by hand. Techniques aren't marked in the standard notation.

**Recorded bass** (`B`) cuts the low end of the centre of the stereo mix, where the bass sits, and leaves the
sides alone. Measured on Sweet Emotion: Cut takes the bass range down about 20 to 25 dB (30 to 150 Hz),
14 dB at 150 to 250 Hz, and leaves everything above 500 Hz as it was.

## The greykit.games standard

PumaBass follows games-meta's `STANDARD.md` for Tactical games:

- §1 the credit, on the title and in About;
- §2 Settings → This browser → Forget everything, which removes only `pumabass*` keys;
- §3 the boot and title screen: `web/js/title.js` carries `canon/gk-title.js` (v0.4) byte for byte. The
  boot is the studio ident, exactly as in every Tactical game; the title is the canon's v0.4 default (the
  name and menu down the left, over the stage), keeping its markup, menu and keys, but **wears
  PumaBass's own look** (below), as do the canon's sheets;
- §4 the palette: `web/style.css` opens with `canon/gk-tokens.css` byte for byte, and PumaBass's look
  is built on those colours;
- §5 storage keys `pumabass_<feature>_v<n>` (prefs, offsets, recent, resume, muted, tuner, favorites, stats);
- §6 the title menu, §7 Settings in order; not §8: songs have no mute (no header switch, and `M` while
  playing is the Mix), by choice, since the game is nothing without its sound. The title's own sounds
  have a switch (below);
- §9 page metadata and icons, §10 `tools/check-assets.js` byte for byte, run by CI's validate stage;
- §11 no native dialogs;
- §14 the integrated intro: `web/js/title.js` also carries `canon/gk-intro.js` (v0.4), byte for byte.
  The boot plays canon's **meow**, ending as the logo does, through the title's Sound switch
  (`web/js/title-sound.js`: `bootMeow`), so a player who turned the title's sound off boots in
  silence too; on a cold load the browser keeps the boot silent, as canon's does. `finish()` is passed
  `prefix: 'pumabass_'`, so the intro's facts in the console include what PumaBass keeps in the
  browser. Then the hold, the crossing, the name a line at a time, the menu,
  and the credit under it, and the **theme** starts: a bass groove played live, looping under the
  title (`title-sound.js`; the loop itself is below). The scene is a painting playing to it: its
  lights on the theme's beat, its crowd bobbing (`painting.js`; the painting is below too). The theme
  fades out when the title goes, and goes quiet while the tuner is up. The title has two switches
  top right: Sound (`M` on the title), the master, for the meow, the ticks (a pick on a muted
  string, climbing the A minor pentatonic down the menu) and the theme; and Music, the theme alone.
  They're saved in the prefs (`titleSound`, `titleMusic`) and touch nothing else. `?hold=always`
  shows the hold.

The deliberate gap: from the title on, PumaBass has its own look rather than the Tactical one: heavy condensed caps (Saira Condensed,
leaning), glossy navy tiles, and a cyan selection bar for whatever you're on, in the greykit palette.
It restyles the canon title and sheets from `web/style.css` (selectors start with `html` to win over the
canon's injected CSS) without touching their copies.
Don't edit the canon copies here; change them in games-meta and copy them back.

## The web demo

greykit.com/games/pumabass/ runs PumaBass with no server: the page alone, playing the demo song.
`tools/build-web.sh` copies the page, its icons and manifest, and `web/` (as `web-<commit>/`) into `public/`, and marks the page
`<html data-edition="web">`. With that mark (`WEB` in `web/js/api.js`) the page never asks the server
for anything: the library is the demo song alone, and what needs the server (your songs, choosing a
folder, Rescan, saving a take) opens a "Not available in the demo" popup (`notInDemo`). Quit isn't
offered, and opening the song doesn't look for the Real Tone Cable (which asks to use the microphone)
until you've chosen an input. Everything the page loads is by relative path, so it works from a subfolder.

`web/` goes up as `web-<commit>/`. greykit.com sits behind Cloudflare, which keeps scripts and styles for a
month: a deploy that reused the same addresses would leave visitors on the old scripts (or a new page with
old scripts). Each deploy's files get addresses Cloudflare has never seen, and the page itself is only kept
a minute. So inside `web/`, find files relative to the script (`new URL('…', import.meta.url)`), never by a
path from the page.

CI's deploy stage builds it and rsyncs `public/` to greykit.com/games/pumabass/ on every main commit, as
the other greykit.games deploy, with the group's DreamHost CI variables. Only the build goes up: never
the server, the tools, the screenshots or any songs. Earlier deploys' `web-<commit>/` folders are left
in place for two days (a page opened before a deploy, or served from Cloudflare's minute, still finds
its own scripts and `audio/copybook.m4a`), then pruned by the next deploy over the same connection.

To try it locally, build it into a subfolder and serve that with any static server:

```bash
bash tools/build-web.sh .web-preview/games/pumabass
python -m http.server 8017 --directory .web-preview
```

then open http://localhost:8017/games/pumabass/.

## Icon and share card

`icon.svg` is drawn by hand. Everything else in the `<head>` is rendered from it:

```bash
bash tools/render-icons.sh
```

That writes the favicons here and runs greykit-meta's `make-assets.sh` for `og.png` and
`apple-touch-icon.png`. It needs headless Chrome or Edge and Python with Pillow.

## The title's painting

`web/art/` is made from a picture (the puma on stage from behind, facing the crowd), which isn't in the
repo; keep it with the project's references:

```bash
python3 tools/make-stage-art.py ~/Claude/references/pumabass.png
```

It turns the picture into hi-bit pixel art (a third of its size, 64 colours, ordered dither), paints in a
third more arena on the left (where the title's menu stands), paints out the picture's crowd and draws a
crowd of animals in its place, paints in the stage behind the puma, and cuts the puma into parts (legs,
body with the bass, head) with the line its tail hangs along, so `painting.js` can bob it, nod it and
swing the tail; and the stage front and a strip of haze, each a layer it draws and plays. It's seeded, so the same picture always gives the same painting. It needs
Python with Pillow and numpy.

## The title's theme

`web/audio/theme.m4a` is made from a recording of a bass groove, played live, which isn't in the repo
either:

```bash
python3 tools/make-theme.py ~/Claude/references/"Bass Groove Live.m4a"
```

It finds the beats (the take holds 111.8 BPM within 15 ms), takes twelve bars from the first downbeat
(where the groove comes back round to its opening), folds the moment after the twelfth bar into the
first so the join doesn't click, and writes it as AAC, with `theme.json` (its beat and how many) for
the painting. `title-sound.js` loops it with Web Audio, which goes round without a gap. It needs
ffmpeg, and Python with numpy.

## Settings file and options

Settings live in `config.json` beside PumaBass, and can be edited by hand:

```json
{ "libraryDir": "/media/me/songs", "port": 8014, "host": "127.0.0.1" }
```

| Key | What | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `libraryDir` | a Rocksmith folder, on its own tab | none: chosen in Settings |
| `port` | the address's port | 8014 |
| `host` | who may connect: `127.0.0.1` is this computer only; `0.0.0.0` lets other devices on the network in (they can then see the folder names in the picker) | `127.0.0.1` |
| `bundlesDir` | the Bundles folder | `bundles/` |
| `cdlcDir` | the CDLC folder | `cdlc/` |
| `musicDir` | the Music folder | `music/` |
| `cacheDir` | decoded audio and the song index | `cache/` |
| `openBrowser` | open a browser on start | `true` |

For one run: `./start.sh --port 8015 --library /path/to/songs --no-open` (start.bat takes the same).

On Windows without Git Bash, unzip `vgmstream-win64.zip` from [vgmstream's releases](https://github.com/vgmstream/vgmstream/releases) into `tools/vgmstream/`. vgmstream can also be on the `PATH`.

## Playing on a TV

On a big screen PumaBass draws everything larger so it reads from the couch; **Settings → Screen →
Computer, up close** turns that off. It keeps the screen awake while open; if a set-top PC still blanks,
switch its screensaver off (e.g. `xset s off -dpms`).

On Linux, `tools/pumabass.service` runs PumaBass as a systemd user service (the steps are in the file);
point a full-screen browser at it, e.g. `chromium --kiosk http://localhost:8014/`. A kiosk browser may
have nowhere to ask for the cable: allow it once in a normal window first, or by policy, with
`{ "AudioCaptureAllowedUrls": ["http://localhost:8014"] }` in
`/etc/chromium/policies/managed/pumabass.json`.

## Timing and scoring, in play

**Sync** (on the menu) lines up the picture, the sound and the cable. **Line up**: a tick sounds while a
bar swings side to side; move the bar (`←` / `→`, `Shift` for bigger steps) until it lands on the tick,
then `Enter`. **Play along** (with the Real Tone Cable): keep the strings quiet through the count-in, then
pluck the open E with the notes; this sets scoring's allowance for the cable and checks the level. `Esc`
leaves with nothing changed. Run it again after changing speakers, headphones or screen.

A note counts when the right note is plucked 200 ms early to 300 ms late (Rocksmith 2014's own windows),
in real time, so slowed practice is judged the same. Chords count on the pluck alone (a wrong shape isn't
caught). Right notes marked missed: tune, then run **Sync** with the cable open.

The tuner aims for the song's tuning, ticks a string once it holds within 3 cents, and opens by itself
when the tuning changes. The bass plays through PumaBass with a short delay; with a splitter to an amp,
turn off **Hear your bass**. **Notes** (beside Speed) thins the chart using Rocksmith's own simpler
levels. A song counts as finished when it's played to its last note with at least half of it actually
played.
